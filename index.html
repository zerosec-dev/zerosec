The Zero Trust Overhead Trap

14 Sep 2026

Every program I work with is under orders to cut overhead. Buying a managed zero trust product is the fastest way to show that on a slide: the billets go away, the training line goes away, and the architecture becomes someone else's problem. The overhead that actually got cut was the understanding.

You find out later, and always at a bad time. The appliance denies traffic mid-exercise and nobody on the floor can name the rule. An assessor asks where the policy decision point is and the honest answer is a support queue in another timezone. That isn't a lean organization, it's a dependent one, and dependency is a security property like any other.

This page is what we do about that. We're engineers, not a licence.

2. The architecture

NIST SP 800-207 with every component inside your boundary. Keycloak and FreeIPA for user identity, with CAC/PIV over mutual TLS and FIDO2 for step-up. osquery and FleetDM for device posture, treated as facts with a timestamp: a stale fact is a failed fact. SPIRE for workload identity, X.509 SVIDs on a one-hour TTL so there is no long-lived credential on disk. WireGuard, Headscale or Nebula for the encrypted overlay, deny by default, no flat L2.

Envoy is the PEP, calling OPA over ext_authz on a unix socket before it forwards anything. OPA is the PE: the trust algorithm is Rego in git, unit tested in CI, signed before it ships to the enclave. Vault issues the PKI and dynamic credentials. Vector ships Envoy access logs and OPA decision logs into ClickHouse, which is where the visibility pillar is actually satisfied rather than claimed.

Cut the uplink and the loop still closes. That is the test, and most of the market fails it.

3. How we work

  • Assessment. Your estate measured against SP 800-207 and the DoD seven pillars. Written findings, gaps ranked by risk, no product attached to the recommendation.
  • Architecture. Target design and migration path: control plane and data plane separated, PEP and PDP placement, the trust algorithm written down in language your team can argue with.
  • Implementation. Built with your engineers, air-gapped first and relaxed later if your environment allows it. You get the repos and the runbooks.
  • Accreditation support. STIG baselines, control mappings, and decision logs that answer a CMMC Level 2 or DFARS 252.204-7012 assessor with evidence instead of narrative.
  • Enablement. Pairing, runbooks, and tabletops until your people can explain the design and restore it without calling anyone.

We charge for engineering time, not licences. If the appliance you already bought is the right call, we will make it work and say so. Real overhead we will happily delete: three directories down to one, manual certificate handling replaced by automatic rotation, tribal memory replaced by runbooks. Understanding is not overhead.
