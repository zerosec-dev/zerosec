2. The architecture

NIST SP 800-207 separates the decision from the enforcement. The policy engine (PE) decides. The policy administrator (PA) issues the session credential. The policy enforcement point (PEP) sits on the data path in front of the resource. Run all three inside your boundary.

Identity

Keycloak issues OIDC tokens. FreeIPA with 389-DS holds accounts, hosts, and groups; Keycloak federates to it over LDAP. Users authenticate with CAC/PIV over mutual TLS. FIDO2 covers contractors without a card and provides step-up. Set access token lifetime to 5–15 minutes. Caveat: group membership is not authorization. Keycloak answers who the subject is; policy decides what that subject may do now.

Device

osquery exposes the endpoint as a SQL database. FleetDM schedules the queries, typically every 300 s, and forwards results to the PE as timestamped facts. Treat a posture row older than two intervals as a failure. Caveat: agents stop reporting, and most deployments then fail open. Alarm on staleness, not only on non-compliance.

Workload and network

SPIRE attests each workload and mints an X.509 SVID with a 1 h TTL, so no long-lived credential sits on disk. WireGuard carries the traffic. Headscale coordinates keys when node churn makes hand-managed peers impractical; Nebula suits hub-and-spoke estates behind NAT. Set reachability to deny by default. Caveat: the overlay is not the authorization boundary. Mesh membership grants a path to a PEP and nothing else. If a leaked WireGuard key reaches a resource, the design has collapsed back to a VPN.

Policy

Envoy runs as a sidecar and as the edge gateway. It verifies the peer SVID, then calls OPA over ext_authz on a unix socket before forwarding. The trust algorithm is Rego in git. Run opa test in CI against recorded inputs. Sign the bundle before it ships to the enclave. Budget about 1.3 ms added to the call path, of which roughly 0.6 ms is policy evaluation; measure your own.

Telemetry

Vector ships Envoy access logs, OPA decision logs, and osquery results to ClickHouse. Sample the access log under load. Never sample the decision log: it is the evidence an assessor asks for.

The test: unplug the uplink, then authenticate a user and call a service. If either fails, the decision does not run inside your boundary.

3. How we work

  • Assessment. We measure the estate against SP 800-207 and the seven DoD pillars and rank the gaps by risk. Output: a findings document with no product attached to the recommendation.
  • Architecture. We separate control plane from data plane, place the PDP and PEPs, and write the trust algorithm down. Output: a target design and a migration path your team can argue with.
  • Implementation. We build it with your engineers, air-gapped first, and relax it later if the environment allows. Output: the repos, the configs, and the runbooks.
  • Accreditation support. We map controls to components and pull decision records from the log store. Output: STIG baselines and CMMC Level 2 / DFARS 252.204-7012 evidence.
  • Enablement. We pair with your operators and run tabletops until they can explain the design and restore it unassisted.
